Sampling & coverage

Data

Where the images come from: how locations were chosen, which panorama was kept at each one, and what the imagery can and can't see.

Sampling design

Jakarta is overlaid with a grid of 50-metre hexagonal cells. Cells are sampled with probabilities that depend on their distance from the city's RPTRA public-space sites, so locations close to a site are deliberately oversampled. Sampling ran in four collection phases (1a, 1b, 2 and 3), and a cell's inclusion probability depends on its phase and distance band.

For every sampled cell and every year from 2013 to 2019, the Street View service (Google) is queried for imagery within 25 metres of the cell's centre. If several panoramas qualify, the one nearest the centre is kept. Each kept panorama is downloaded as six images at headings of 0°, 60°, 120°, 180°, 240° and 300°, covering the full 360° view.

What the imagery can and can't see

Coverage

Only where the camera car drives

Lanes too narrow for a vehicle, roadless areas and restricted zones are rarely photographed. Coverage falls from about half of cells within 100 m of a site to about a fifth beyond 2 km. Estimates therefore describe the vehicle-accessible street network.

Weighting

Correcting for how cells were chosen

Each observation is weighted by the inverse of the probability that its cell was both sampled and returned imagery. The second probability is estimated from distance band, phase and road characteristics. Weights can't recover places Street View never visits.

Timing

Daylight snapshots

Each panorama is one instant, captured in daylight and dated only to month and year. It records who is visible at that moment, which is a measure of presence weighted by time spent in view, not a count of trips.

Building the image set

The team downloaded the images in batches across the four phases and stored them as Kaggle datasets for GPU inference. Assembling them into one clean table took its own engineering:

Rule

Never trust a stored file path

Paths recorded at download time break as soon as folders move, and some were blank or pointed at files that no longer existed. Every image location is rebuilt from its panorama ID, year and heading instead.

Rule

Normalise IDs before joining

Some panorama IDs carried a trailing date suffix that made them silently fail to match the master list. Stripping it before the join recovered capture months for almost all of them.

Rule

De-duplicate across batches

Overlapping batch ranges meant some images were downloaded and processed more than once. Duplicates are flagged and removed on a unique key of panorama, year and heading.
