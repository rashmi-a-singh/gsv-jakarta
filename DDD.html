Evaluation Memo

DDD Validation

The gender-recognition pipeline was run over 31,074 hand-annotated Street View images collected by Digital Data Divide (DDD)[cite: 7]. This page compares the machine's output, panorama by panorama, against human annotations across 4,765 panoramas from 2013–2019[cite: 7].

Executive Summary

The headline finding is that the aggregate ratio and the individual classifications give opposite verdicts, and only the first is favourable[cite: 7].

  • Aggregate Ratio: At a threshold of τ = 0.6275, the machine reports a male-to-female ratio of 2.85–2.97 against a hand-labelled truth of 2.944[cite: 7]. (Note: at the newly confirmed τ = 0.62, the ratio is 2.75–2.86[cite: 7]). This is the first independent check that the threshold has ever received[cite: 7].
  • Per-Person Accuracy: Individual agreement over the same panoramas is poor[cite: 7]. The machine finds only 53% of the women the annotators recorded[cite: 7]. When the machine reports a woman, 42.5% of the time the annotator saw none[cite: 7].

The ratio is correct because the errors offset, not because the individual classification is highly accurate[cite: 7].

The Ratio Agrees

This comparison evaluates two scenarios: Scenario A (excluding bad_quality boxes, leaving 3,757 people) and Scenario B (retaining bad_quality boxes, leaving 6,810 people)[cite: 7].

Threshold (τ) Machine M:F (Scenario A) Machine M:F (Scenario B)
0.57 2.02 2.14
0.59 2.27 2.38
0.62 2.75 2.86
0.6275 2.85 2.97
0.65 3.21 3.32

Hand labels on these same panoramas give a ratio of 2.944[cite: 7]. While the threshold was originally fitted to an assumed field ratio, it now survives contact with independent evidence[cite: 7].

The Classifications Do Not

Decomposing the count error for women under Scenario B at the original 0.6275 benchmark reveals substantial unreliability in both directions[cite: 7]:

  • Annotators recorded 3,241 women; the machine reported 1,717[cite: 7].
  • 2,527 women were missed entirely across 1,262 panoramas[cite: 7].
  • 1,003 women were reported where the annotator recorded fewer or none[cite: 7].

The net of −2,527 and +1,003 is what produces 53% of the hand total, netting to approximately the right ratio[cite: 7]. Men show the same undercount (53.4%) but far cleaner positives (only 14.8% false)[cite: 7]. Furthermore, as the threshold rises to improve the aggregate ratio, per-person agreement (Cohen's κ) monotonically declines[cite: 7].

Where the People Go (The Funnel)

The single most useful decomposition shows that the pipeline finds people and then discards them based on exclusion rules that have never been validated against hand labels[cite: 7]. Over the same 4,765 panoramas[cite: 7]:

  • Everyone the annotators saw: 16,462 (including 12,784 adults whose gender could be identified)[cite: 7].
  • People the model detected: 30,901 (The detector found 188% of everyone the annotators counted)[cite: 7].
  • After the rider gate: 10,494 remaining[cite: 7].
  • After the confidence gate: 6,810 remaining[cite: 7].
  • After the size gate: 3,757 remaining[cite: 7].

Ultimately, the gender model only ever sees 12% to 22% of the people the detector originally located[cite: 7]. A claim that "the model misses women" is incomplete; the pipeline finds people but discards them via gates[cite: 7].
